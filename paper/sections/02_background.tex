\section{Background and related work}\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{OPD as policy optimization with a token reward.} Let $\piS$ be the student and
$\piT$ the teacher quantized to $b$ bits. For a prompt $x$ and a student sample
$y\sim\piS(\cdot\mid x)$, define the per-token reward
\begin{equation}
  r_t = \log \piT(y_t \mid x, y_{<t}) - \log \piS(y_t \mid x, y_{<t}).
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
Sampled-token reverse-KL distillation minimizes
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\piS}\Big[\sum_t \sg\big(r_t\big)\,
  \log\piS(y_t\mid x, y_{<t})\Big],
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
a policy-gradient estimator of $\mathrm{KL}(\piS\,\|\,\piT)$ in which the reward is held fixed
(stop-gradient, $\sg$). The teacher enters only through $\log\piT$ on student-written tokens,
which is why its precision can matter even when its own generations look unaffected.
Variants replace the sampled-token estimator with a KL over the teacher's top-$k$ tokens, clip
$r_t$ \citep{reopold2026}, add a trust region \citep{trustregionopd2026}, or precompute teacher
log-probabilities to remove the live teacher \citep{lightningopd2026}. A survey organizes these
choices by feedback signal, teacher access and loss granularity \citep{opdsurvey2026};
\citet{easyopd2026} provide a modular framework for comparing them.

\paragraph{When the teacher's signal is uninformative.} A wide student--teacher gap can make the
token signal uninformative; \citet{understandingopd2026} propose an informativeness measure that
we reuse as one of our fidelity metrics. \citet{rethinkingopd2026} find that alignment of
thinking patterns matters more than raw teacher strength and that an off-policy cold start
fixes many OPD failures; we therefore start every condition from the same cold-start student.

\paragraph{Quantization formats.} We study the formats a desktop practitioner would use for the
teacher: FP8 weights and activations (W8A8), NVIDIA's 4-bit floating-point format (NVFP4),
4-bit integer weights with activation-aware scaling \citep[AWQ, W4A16, group 128;][]{lin2024awq},
3-bit integer weights from second-order rounding \citep[GPTQ, group 128;][]{frantar2023gptq}, and
optionally a 2-bit llama.cpp k-quant \citep[Q2\_K;][]{llamacpp}. All quantized rungs share one
calibration set (Section~\ref{sec:setup-ladder}).
